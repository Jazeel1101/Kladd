# Overlay placement

How the logging overlay grows out of the pill, and how it knows what the student writes in. Entry point: `LoggingOverlay` in `packages/core/src/logging/LoggingOverlay.ts`, opened when a session ends, by the logging-mode pill's star (`toggle`) and gear (`toggleSettings`), or by "Show logging overlay" in the pill menu (for trying adapters).

1. **The pill says where it rests.** Only the pill's renderer knows where its notch is, so `useNotchDrag` sends a `PillDock` (the notch's screen rect and its wall, null while floating) through `SetPillDock` whenever it comes to rest, and `null` when it starts moving.
2. **Placement.** `placeBesidePill` in `packages/domain/src/Placement.ts` picks the side: right of a left-docked pill, left of a right-docked one, above a bottom-docked one; a floating pill grows it toward the side with more room, across its long side. The panel's side touches the notch's long side, centred on it and moved only to stay in the display's work area. Before the pill has rested, `placeWithoutPill` centres it at the right screen edge, unjoined. It also returns the `PillJoin`: the side of the panel the pill is on and the span the notch covers, in the overlay's window.
3. **One shape from two windows.** The overlay's window lies one level under the pill's and is larger than its panel by `overlayMargin` on every side, so on the pill's side it reaches under the notch. `apps/ui/src/logging/neck.ts` draws the neck there: a strip under the notch's side with inverted corners flaring into the panel. The notch hides the strip; the flares, and the strip where the notch's corners round away, join the two in the same `liquidColor`. The window has no macOS shadow: the panel draws its own, and the neck lies over it, without one, so nothing darkens the join; the pill drops its own shadow while joined, for the same reason. The window lets clicks through everywhere but the panel and neck (`SetClickThrough`).
4. **Opening.** Core sets the new target and join with `open: false`, shows the window (`showInactive()`, so the writing app keeps keyboard focus), then sets `open: true`; the UI then springs the panel out of the middle of the join with a scale and fade, which the compositor runs without repainting (a `clip-path` or `filter` animation repaints the whole panel every frame, and lags). While open, a new `PillDock` moves the window without animation, and `null` drops the neck until the pill rests again.
5. **Focus.** The overlay has nothing to type into, so its window is not focusable: clicking or scrolling it never takes keyboard focus from the writing app.
6. **Writing target.** On open, the `FocusTracker` port returns a `FocusSnapshot`, and `adapterFor` picks the adapter for the front app's bundle id, else `Generic`, whose `target` reports the `WritingTarget` the header shows: app and document, by default the app name and window title. `FocusTrackerDarwin` runs `kladd-capture focus` (`packages/platform-darwin/native/Sources/KladdCapture/Focus.swift`), which reads `NSWorkspace` and Accessibility, including the selected rows of the window's lists (`selection`). Without Accessibility only the app is known; if the helper breaks, the target is empty. Accessibility is asked for once a session has started (`askForAccessibility` in `Sessions.start`), never waited on.
7. **Following.** While the overlay is open, core reads `FocusTracker.watch` and updates the target when the adapter reports a new one (another OneNote page). `kladd-capture watch-focus` subscribes to the app's AX notifications, prints a snapshot after each burst (150 ms debounce), and exits when the app quits or its stdin closes. Closing the overlay interrupts the watch.

Electron and AX both use points with the origin at the top-left of the main display, so rects pass through unchanged. Displays left of or above the main one have negative coordinates.
