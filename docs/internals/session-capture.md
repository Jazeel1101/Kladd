# Session capture on macOS

`SessionCaptureDarwin` runs one `kladd-capture session` per work session (`native/Sources/KladdCapture/Session.swift`) and provides `SessionCapture`, `ClipCapture` and `SessionHelper`: the same process records the session's clips (`Clip.swift`, `Ink.swift`) on `clip-start <folder>` and `clip-stop` lines on its stdin, takes the ⌃⇧C screenshot on `screenshot <file>` (`ScreenCaptureDarwin` asks `SessionHelper` first), and answers on stdout (usage in `main.swift`). They write the live files of the session folder and of a clip's folder (`packages/domain/src/SessionFolder.ts`). Starting and stopping the session work like `record` (`docs/internals/screen-capture.md`); a clip with no session running fails.

## Session

- **No video or sound.** Those are clips'. t = 0 is the `start` event.
- **Starts without a display.** While the Mac is locked ScreenCaptureKit lists none; the session starts anyway and takes keyframes once the privacy filter can be built (tried every second).
- **`frame` events are written with the JPEG**, before OCR, so a crash during OCR still leaves the keyframe on the timeline. `ocr` names the `.txt` OCR will write; it is missing when OCR failed or had not finished. At stop, OCR gets 10 s more. Vision's first accurate-mode call on a Mac loads its model, which can take 30–100 s.
- **Stop keeps watching files for 1.5 s.** fseventsd hands a change over up to about a second late, and `FSEventStreamFlushSync` does not hurry it; changes that arrive then get the `stop` event's `t`.

## Clip

- **One clock.** t = 0 is the host-clock time of the clip's first video frame, the clock ScreenCaptureKit stamps frames with; marks use it too. The microphone has its own clock, so t = 0 is converted to it, and a microphone that starts late is padded with silence. Players ignore the empty edit AVAssetWriter would insert, and speech would drift against `t`.
- **Video for watching and for frames.** The whole main display, fit into 1920×1200 (about its size in points, so text is as sharp as on screen and a frame fits an LLM's image without scaling), 30 fps so ink and the pointer move smoothly, H.264 at 4 Mbit/s with a keyframe every 2 s so any `t` decodes fast. ScreenCaptureKit sends no frames while the screen is still, so a still screen costs little; the last frame is appended again at stop so the video lasts until `stop`.
- **AVAssetWriter, not `SCRecordingOutput`.** `record` uses `SCRecordingOutput`; the clip writes frames itself to control the bitrate and use that clock. The files are fragmented every 10 s, so after a crash `screen.mp4` and `audio.m4a` still play up to the last fragment. No B-frames: with them the fragmented writer failed (-16341) right after its first fragment. A stream that stops on its own is started again into the same file after 1 s (at once, ScreenCaptureKit's next call can hang), and a start that has sent no frame after 3 s is given up for a new one. A clip with no frame 10 s after `clip-start`, or stopped before its first frame, ends as failed, without a video; core then deletes its folder, so the next clip takes its name. A clip can't start while the Mac is locked.
- **One ScreenCaptureKit process at a time.** Two helpers of the same app break each other's capture: a helper that has called `SCShareableContent` and is still running makes every stream of another fail with -3805 ("application connection being interrupted") within milliseconds, and its own call can hang for ever. So the session's helper records its clips and takes the ⌃⇧C screenshot, through the session's privacy filter and display; a screenshot during a clip leaves no gap in `screen.mp4`. Only with no session running is the screenshot its own `screenshot` process.
- **A clip never stops the session.** A clip whose video, sound or ink fails answers `clip-failed`, and the session goes on. Its sound waits for the writer to be ready, silence before a late microphone included: appending to a writer that is not ready aborts the whole helper.
- **Ink, as in Scrawl.** Hold ⇧ alone and move the pointer: a red stroke on a click-through panel over the main display, which fades out 0.65–1.3 s after ⇧ is let go. ⇧ pressed with ⌘, ⌃ or ⌥ (as in ⌥⇧C) draws nothing until it is let go, and a ⇧ press that moves less than 6 pt (typing a capital) is no stroke. Input is polled modifier flags and the pointer position at 120 Hz, which need no permission; Input Monitoring is only for reading keys. The panel ignores the mouse and never activates, and the helper is not left out of the capture, so ink is in `screen.mp4` as it looked. `KLADD_TEST_INK=1` draws one scripted stroke from t = 1 s to 2 s through the same code, to check that without real input.
- **Microphone.** If the permission was never asked, the clip asks and starts without sound; sound starts when it is granted, padded from t = 0. macOS asks on behalf of Kladd, the responsible app, so Kladd's `Info.plist` needs `NSMicrophoneUsageDescription` (`apps/desktop/electron-builder.yml`). With the lid closed, a MacBook's built-in microphone delivers no samples, so `audio.m4a` is missing.

## Privacy

The lists come from domain as JSON arguments (`--exclude-apps`, `--deny-paths`). The session adds Kladd's data dir itself, as `<dir>/../..`, because the folder is `<dataDir>/recordings/<session>`. The filter (`Privacy.swift`) is rebuilt when an excluded app starts, since ScreenCaptureKit only excludes apps that are running; from its launch until a filter that leaves it out is in use, no keyframes or clip frames are written, so a clip's video freezes briefly. The titles of excluded apps are left out of `focus` events.
